# Product requirements

## Problem and audience

Professional drivers need a quick record of what they spent on their vehicle and when it needs attention. Start with ride-hailing car drivers in one recruitable Iranian area. Tehran is the working assumption. Notes, receipts, spreadsheets, memory, and existing expense apps are the comparison points. The product hypothesis is that drivers will repeatedly log real costs when the workflow is fast and the monthly view is useful.

## Required outcomes

1. A driver can install a signed APK, add a catalog or manual vehicle, and log a real fuel or operating expense in Persian with limited effort.
2. A driver can review and correct records, understand the current Jalali month total, see the latest recorded odometer, and set personal maintenance reminders.
3. Core logging works offline. Driver-entered service history is presented as self-reported. User-defined intervals are labeled accordingly.
4. Drivers can explicitly export and retain a portable backup at M4. From M5, invited drivers can opt in to recovery from their last acknowledged cloud snapshot.
5. The closed pilot measures activation, repeat logging, and quick-add friction before expansion. Business research runs alongside M2–M4 without a directory or partner application.

## Release decisions

M4 is a usable offline prototype. M6 is the market-ready closed pilot, with recovery, privacy controls, signed distribution, and support. M7 produces the driver evidence needed for the next scope decision. The plan establishes the requirements and decision rules. Pilot outcomes remain unverified.
